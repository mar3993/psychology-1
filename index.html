THE LANGUAGE OF AGE

There is no birthday that makes someone “officially old.”

In everyday language, middle age is often placed around 40–64 and older adulthood around 65 and beyond. But those are social shortcuts, not medical verdicts. Different countries, services and communities draw their lines differently.

Everyday language: Middle age is often understood as roughly 40–64. “Older adult” often begins around 65—but people use the words differently.

HEALTHY AGEING, YOUR WAY

Health is more than a number on a calendar.

The World Health Organization describes healthy ageing as maintaining the ability to do what matters. These ideas can look different for every person and every life.

01Move in ways that feel goodWalking, gardening, dancing or strength work: the useful activity is the one that fits your life.
02Keep close to peopleRelationships, community and shared interests can support wellbeing across the years.
03Make space to keep learningCuriosity, choice and contribution are all part of a meaningful life.
